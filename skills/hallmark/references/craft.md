# Craft

The file every page build reads, beside `imagery.md` (and `layout.md` on a theme build). Type, colour, surfaces, motion, copy, section openings, slots and finish, as numbers wherever a number exists; `layout.md` owns the canvas, the sizes, the room, the sections and the hero. The theme file supplies the material; these two files supply the rules it sits in. Gate numbers in parentheses point at `gates.md`.

## 1. Type

Type carries the page. At most two families, a display face and a body face, which may be one family. A third is one **outlier** register (a wordmark, a headline figure, code) and it carries exactly one role (mono for code and for measured figures counts as one); a second role makes it a third family in running use (37, F13). Same family at different weights is one family.

- **Extremes read as chosen.** Three weights at most across the page, display and body together (F19), picked as extremes: 300 beside 800, or 400 beside 700.
- **A ratio, not increments.** 1.25 by default (1.333 or 1.5 for more drama), built from a 16px body. The sizes in running use are the roles in `layout.md` § 2 (hero head, section head, item title, lead, body, label, and the smaller display step for a long head or a statement), plus a wordmark size if the theme names one; hierarchy past those comes from weight and colour.
- **Display:** tracking -0.02 to -0.04em on a regular-width face, 0 to -0.015em on a condensed or very heavy one (they are drawn tight already; two letters touching is a fault, not a style), line-height 1.0 to 1.15 (tighter as it grows; 1.2 reads as inherited; a mixed-case poster head may close to 0.92). Uppercase heads that can wrap never go under 1.0 (55). A head is one weight, one style and one colour throughout; one word set apart by italic, colour, weight or a highlight is the accented word, the field's most-named tell after the eyebrow (38). Stress comes from a line break or a second line at a second size. Default display size `clamp(2.75rem, 5vw + 1rem, 5.25rem)`; cap 5.5rem, 6rem on display-heavy themes, 7rem only for a single word; a poster-scale theme or shape (Manifesto, Marquee Hero, a Poster hero; Broadside fits its lines to the measure by its own rule, and an HP2 overflow head is sized to bleed) sizes with the screen up to the container's cap, `clamp(4rem, 10vw, 9rem)`, so it reaches full size at 1440 and holds there at 1920 where the container holds (Floor 14), while the fold still holds the headline, one sentence and the action. A wide face (Ultra, Syne, a wide Anybody) takes the poster scale a step down, or a four-word head breaks into four lines.
- **Body:** 16px minimum (14px never for body), line-height 1.5 to 1.65, measure 45 to 75ch (`max-width: 65ch`) (25). Never all-caps body, never justified without hyphenation, never tracking above 0.05em on body.
- **Labels, captions, table heads, meta, tags, chips, nav links:** the body face, a step smaller, sentence case (or lowercase on a theme whose voice is lowercase), untracked (67). Never uppercase, never small caps, never letter-spaced. Mono only when the content is code, a figure or a measurement, or when mono is the theme's material (64). Capitals belong to a wordmark and to a theme whose display voice is uppercase; there a control may follow the display's case at 0.04em of tracking or less.
- **Headline sizing by copy length.** Count the rendered `h1`: 20 characters or fewer, full display (a single word may grow to 7rem); 21 to 50, display; 51 to 90, one step down (`--text-display-s`) or split at the sense break with `<br>`; over 90, rewrite. Self-written headlines aim for seven words and 50 characters.
- **Heading measure rides on the element**, in `ch`: a theme's scaffold sets `h1` 20ch, `h2` 30ch, `h3` 40ch (a custom page sets its own); a head at display-s takes about 24ch. Never a `max-width` on the heading group's wrapper.
- **Required:** `font-display: swap`; a fallback stack of the same build (a grotesk falls back to `system-ui`, a serif to Georgia), with `size-adjust` and the overrides only where the face's metrics are published, never guessed; `font-variant-numeric: tabular-nums` on any figures that compare; `text-wrap: balance` on heads, `pretty` on ledes and body; curly quotes, a true ellipsis, a non-breaking space inside unit pairs (`10 MB`, `⌘ K`) in a proportional face (in mono the unit sits outside the mono span or takes a plain space, since a mono space is a full cell). No `--` and no em or en dash anywhere in rendered copy; a comma, a colon or a full stop does the work.
- **Wordmark.** May take a different face from the body; on tone-rich themes it should. Collapse to one family only when the body voice is the brand (a letter, a manifesto, a long document).
- **Load only the weights you use.** Never synthesise bold or italic.

Font sourcing for a custom run is in `fonts.md`; a catalog theme has already named its faces.

## 2. Colour

- **OKLCH everywhere.** Perceptual lightness is what makes contrast predictable.
- **Four layers.** Paper (light: L 92 to 98, chroma 0.005 to 0.015 toward the anchor hue; dark: L 12 to 18, 0.008 to 0.015). Ink (light paper: L 16 to 24; dark paper: L 88 to 96; tinted 0.005 to 0.014). Five to nine neutrals between them, each tinted 0.005 to 0.018 (22). One accent at chroma 0.12 to 0.22 with a paired `--color-accent-ink` (41). A focus colour: the accent's hue, chroma 0.18 to 0.22, L within 5% of the accent.
- **Never a pure absolute** as a base surface or as an improvised value (7). Tint the greys toward the anchor: a warm accent wants warm neutrals.
- **The accent is a signal, not a fill.** Under 5% of any viewport under the default Restrained posture (23): an active nav item, a focus ring, a link underline on hover, the primary control, the one series a chart argues from. If you want more, that is the reflex.
- **Postures.** Restrained (above). **Committed:** one colour carries 30 to 60% of the surface through `--color-field` or the paper family; the accent proper stays a signal. **Full palette:** three or four named roles (field, signal, seal, ink), each with a job and a footprint. **Drenched:** the surface is the colour; ink and rules derive from it. A posture is declared in the stamp before code; a declared surface colour is not accent footprint.
- **Contrast.** Body 4.5:1 (target 7:1) or APCA Lc 60; large text, icons, focus rings and component boundaries 3:1 or Lc 45 (40). Where you cannot render, keep the two lightnesses 15% or more apart. Every rule that paints a dark surface swaps its text colour in the same rule (41). Grey text never sits on a coloured surface: tint it from the hue or use paper.
- **Bans:** gradients of two or three stops only, and never purple-blue, purple-pink, cyan-on-dark (2a); no glow shadows on dark, no chromatic halos or spotlights (29); no alpha as a colour's definition (alpha modifies overlays and shadows); no red-green as the only signal.
- **Dark mode** is opt-in and derived, never inverted: paper 12 to 18, ink 92 to 96, elevation lighter by about 3% L per level, accent chroma down 0.02 to 0.04 with L up 5 to 10, hue fixed. `dark-mode.md` has the per-token table when both modes are asked for.

## 3. Surfaces and detail

Space, the grid, section anatomy and the hero are `layout.md`. What stays here is what a surface is made of.

- **A primary axis.** Left-biased, right-biased, top-heavy or bottom-weighted. Centre-biased is a default, not a choice; at most two centred elements in a hero, one breaking alignment (6b).
- **Spacing is a scale.** 4pt base, ten steps, named by size: `--space-3xs` 2px · `2xs` 4 · `xs` 8 · `sm` 12 · `md` 16 · `lg` 24 · `xl` 40 · `2xl` 64 · `3xl` 96 · `4xl` 144. Raw lengths where a token exists are a Floor failure (24a); at most three optical nudges off the scale (24b). `gap` for siblings, `margin` only to break the flow.
- **Grids.** CSS Grid for the page, Flexbox for component internals; named areas for complex layouts. Flex children that must shrink get `min-width: 0`; icons, logos and images get `flex-shrink: 0`. A grid has no empty cell and no orphan: a last row of one under a fuller row, or six logos as five and one, is a count to change (F20). Grids hold their column count above 1440, and visuals sized in `vw` carry a `max-width` (44d).
- **The surface ladder.** Whitespace, then a hairline rule (1px `--color-rule`, inset or full-bleed), then a tinted well (`--color-paper-2` or `-3`, no border), then a card (a border or a shadow, never a hairline under a wide soft shadow, F24; reserved for content that is independently interactive or of a different kind). Never white cards on a tinted page; never a card in a card, and never a bordered card or label box inside a well: a well holds rows, figures or a visual (4); never jump to the top rung (9, F7). Sibling items in a shared context (stats, KPIs) separate with a top border or a vertical hairline: the first item has no leading padding, the last no trailing, and the rule pattern is reset at every breakpoint where the column count changes.
- **Depth is weight and scale**, not shadow. One shadow if any: whisper `0 1px 2px oklch(20% 0.01 <hue> / 0.05)` or hairline `0 0 0 1px oklch(30% 0.01 <hue> / 0.06)`. Never a border and a wide soft shadow on the same box; commit to one. Elevated surfaces are never darker than their canvas. Eight named z levels: base 1, raised 10, dropdown 100, sticky 200, sticky-nav 300, modal 400, toast 500, tooltip 600; never a freestyle z (56).
- **Page-edge clipping.** `html, body { overflow-x: clip }` on every page (34); `clip` keeps `sticky` and `fixed` working where `hidden` breaks them. The section that holds a deliberately overflowing element keeps `overflow: visible`.
- **Radii.** One radius per level; nested radii are concentric: `inner = outer - padding`, clamped at 2px. Image and screenshot radii scale with the viewport: `min(1vw, var(--radius-card))`.
- **Icons.** One library per project, one stroke width and one style (outline or filled) per page; sizes 16, 20, 24, 32 at the set's natural grid (a 24 viewBox at 24px, never scaled to 19); `currentColor`; beside multi-line text align to the first line; never inside a coloured coin or tile (3, F5); inline with small text use the 16px size. Buttons and badges with an icon take asymmetric padding: the icon side's padding equals the vertical padding.

## 4. The hero's motion

The hero's size, posture and composition are `layout.md` § 6; what it shows is `imagery.md` § 1.

- **Entrance:** one orchestrated settle of 0.4 to 0.8s including its stagger, from an already-visible default; a fade with an 8px rise is one motion. Never a fade plus a sweep plus a scale on one element; never bounce; never scroll-fade on every section; never a starfield, a mouse-parallax or an auto-rotating carousel.
- **Before the hero ships**, its visual answers yes to all of these: it shows the real thing (the user's picture, the product, a library photograph of the brief's subject); it is big (`layout.md` § 6); it keeps the accent under its footprint; under 2 MB with a reduced-motion fallback. Any no, and the visual changes, never the copy.

## 5. Motion

- **Who decides.** The theme's Motion section; a shape's reveal runs only where the theme allows one, and the genre's default holds only where the theme is silent.
- **End states.** Under `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` every reveal shows its end state at once, and the render tier shoots that way, so a rule that draws in on scroll must already be drawn there.
- **Only `transform` and `opacity`** (10). Blur, `clip-path`, mask and shadow may join when they stay smooth on the element alone.
- **Three easings, tokenised:** `--ease-out: cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` for things arriving, `--ease-in: cubic-bezier(0.7, 0, 0.84, 0)` for things leaving, `--ease-in-out: cubic-bezier(0.65, 0, 0.35, 1)` for toggles. The browser's `ease` and `linear` (outside progress) read as uncrafted; overshoot on UI state is dated (12).
- **Three durations:** `--dur-micro` 120ms (press, tick, colour), `--dur-short` 220ms (hover, tooltip, menu), `--dur-long` 420ms (modal, drawer, accordion, page reveal). Exits run 60 to 75% of the enter. Stagger 30 to 50ms between rows, 60 to 100ms between blocks, 500ms of stagger at most, one-shot, never re-fired on scroll.
- **One reveal per page**, staggered by a `--i` custom property, the whole settle 0.4 to 0.8s, and the content is visible without it (62): scope the hidden state to a `.js` class the script adds, or animate from visible with `@starting-style`. Under reduced motion the scaffold's base ends every transition and animation at once; what should still run (a loader, a 150ms fade) takes `.keep-motion`.
- **Scroll:** IntersectionObserver for reveals and for any state that flips at a threshold; a passive scroll listener, throttled to one read per frame, only where direction matters (a bar that hides on the way down); reveal once; scroll-scrubbed motion exists only as the named patterns in `scroll-choreography.md`, one per page (F8).
- **State:** hover is one signal (11) and raises contrast against the rest state: a colour step, or a `translateY(-1px)` lift at micro on a control that keeps its colour, active back to 0; menus `--ease-out` open and `--ease-in` close with the Popover API; modals scale 0.96 to 1 with a backdrop fade over `--dur-long` using `<dialog>` and `inert`; accordions animate `grid-template-rows: 0fr` to `1fr`; tooltips delay 800 to 1000ms on hover and 0ms on focus (17); focus rings never animate (15).
- **Never:** parallax, custom cursors, cursor followers, a marquee that will not pause on hover and focus (18), `will-change` on a whole class, an image that scales or rotates on hover (11), a pulsing dot (a live dot holds still, or blinks once when its value updates), a caret that blinks past five beats, and infinite loops, except a loader, one small loop of 4s or less a cycle, and one ambient loop the theme declares as its material (8s or slower); every loop stops under reduced motion and while off-screen, and one that runs past 5 seconds carries a visible pause control (WCAG 2.2.2).

Full recipes (buttons, inputs, validation, toasts, modals, menus, drag, command palette, optimistic updates) are in `microinteractions.md`; load it when the page has stateful UI. Libraries: CSS and `@property` first, Motion for orchestrated entrances in React, GSAP only when a timeline is the product.

## 6. Copy

Words are design material, and they are where a generated page gives itself away first. Write as the owner would say it across a counter: the brief's own nouns, a real place, a number the user gave, the thing the product does in the verb a customer would use.

- **The swap test.** Every head must fail on a competitor's page. *Software that works for you* fits anyone; *Invoices that chase themselves* fits one product. If a line could move to another site unchanged, rewrite it with a noun only this brief has.
- **Three tests a line:** can the reader picture it, could it be proven false, could only this business say it. A headline on a photograph-led page may simply name what the picture shows (*Batch 14, bottled Friday*).
- **Specific beats clever:** the place, the hour, the material, the input, the result. *Sourdough at four, gone by ten.* *Every tide in the UK, a year ahead.* *Your stems, split in under a minute.* A pun or a riddle is a second-draft move, once a page at most.
- **The subhead** says what it is, who it is for and the one difference, in one sentence of twelve words or fewer. **Section heads** say what the visitor will see or get (*Three loaves a morning*, *From the field to the log*), never a category label (*Features*, *Our story*, *Why us*); a staged shape's one-word stage names (*Schools*, *Housing*) are the exception, and nav links stay plain words.
- **Rhythm.** Mix a short line with a longer one; verbs over adjectives; one idea a sentence; no triads of adjectives (*fast, simple, powerful*).
- **Budget** (58): headlines seven words or fewer, section heads six or fewer; section intros one line; paragraphs two short sentences; lists stop at four items of a three-word label and a ten-word line (five when they are demands or steps that cannot merge; past that it is a table); tables at five rows; a set the user gave keeps its true count; 450 visible words on a page at most, and fewer is fine. Read only the headings and the hero: if they tell the story, the rest is the right length.
- **Refuse the generated voice** (19): *Built for the modern team*, *Unleash*, *Empower*, *Reimagine*, *Supercharge*, *Elevate*, *Unlock*, *seamless*, *robust*, *effortless*, *next-generation*, *world-class*, *In today's landscape*, *Whether you're X or Y*, *It's not just X, it's Y*, *Say goodbye to*, *game-changer*, *journey*, a colon title (*Focus: the app for*), and the rebuttal cadence (*Not a feature. A platform.*) more than once.
- **Buttons:** the verb of the action (`Book a fitting`, `Order a box`); `Continue` only as a flow's second step. **Errors:** what happened, why if known, what to do; no *Oops*, no exclamation marks. **Empty states:** what is empty, why it matters, one action. **Loading:** a spinner after 150 to 300ms, at least 300ms, beside the label; *Loading…* past 2s.
- **Never** placeholder people (Jane Doe), startup clichés (Acme, Nexus), *Click here*, *Enter your email below*, *Quietly trusted by*, a version string in marketing copy, or a number the user did not give (46a).
- **Sample data** inside a built product uses the reserved `example.com`, the field names the brief gives and values that read as samples (`Example Kitchen`, round plausible amounts); never a figure a visitor could take as a claim.
- **Punctuation is typeset:** curly quotes, `…`, a proper apostrophe, no `--`, no em or en dash (a comma, a colon, a full stop or parentheses instead), non-breaking spaces before units. Periods on every standalone sentence, none on list items.

## 7. Opening a section

The reflex is a small tracked-caps label above the heading, and the reflex is the tell (54). The question is *what does the reader not yet know?* If nothing, open with nothing: a generous margin and a heading that starts flush is the most under-used opening there is. If something, it rides on a fact, a destination or an identifier, never on a name for what comes next. Twelve ways in:

1. **Nothing.** Air above, heading flush. Never goes stale.
2. **The heading announces itself.** Move the subject into its first two words. Usually the best move; the label existed because the heading was vague.
3. **A lead-in inside the paragraph**, in a heavier weight, never on its own line.
4. **A drop cap.** One per page, first paragraph only.
5. **A rule that does more than rule**: hairline, fleuron, double rule, colour edge, or pure gap; one divider language down the page, varied in weight and placement from seam to seam (9).
6. **A numeral at graphic scale**, one per page at most and never in the hero by habit: a figure the page argues from at `clamp(6rem, 16vw, 14rem)`, only when the number is real and is the point (an issue, a year, a price that is the offer). A page of giant numbers is a spreadsheet.
7. **A real `<ol>`** with `counter()` when the sequence is genuine.
8. **A caption below** the content instead of above it: a plate in a catalogue, not a slide title.
9. **Marginalia** beside the body in the outer margin.
10. **A change of paper.** The band's surface changes; that edge is the break. No words.
11. **A running head or folio**: continuous chrome at the page edge (a sticky folio, a rail), legal because it persists rather than announces.
12. **Persistence**: the heading stays in view while its content scrolls beneath.

Two moves can share a section; three is noise. Pick one opening and repeat it, or two and alternate; rotate against the log so one opening does not become your signature. Still legal, because each names something present: a `<figcaption>`, a `<th>`, a `<label>`, a `<legend>`, a nav or tab label, a byline or a `<time>` dateline, a unit after its figure, a code block's real filename, a live `role="status"` chip, one linked announcement that goes somewhere real, a plan name (which usually wants to be the heading).

## 8. Slots and missing facts

Pictures, slots and the built product are `imagery.md` § 1 to § 4: a slot always shows something, the user's image replaces it without touching the layout, and the handoff lists every stand-in.

**Text slots.** A missing fact (a venue, a date, an act, a price) is a text slot, never an invented value: `<span class="fill" data-fill="venue">Venue to be announced</span>`, set in the body face in `--color-muted` on a faint tint of it (`background: color-mix(in oklch, var(--color-muted) 14%, transparent)`, 2px radius, no underline) so it reads as a blank to fill, never as a link, at most three on a page, each listed in the handoff with what belongs there. A typeface the page is about but the user has not supplied is a font slot: the display token names it first, a free face of the same build stands in and says so in one caption (stamp `waive 37`), and the handoff asks for the files. A section the user named but gave nothing for is its heading, one line on what will go there and one text slot, or it merges into a neighbour; never a block of pending rows.

## 9. Finish

The last 2% that separates set from generated. sloplint's F-checks report most of it; answer each one, never ship it unread.

- **Controls.** One filled primary per page, dialogs counted as their own page (F4); secondaries outlined or typographic and never higher contrast than the primary; navbar actions quieter than the hero's action. Marketing controls 44 to 52px tall; app UI 32 to 40px with a 44px hit target via an inset pseudo-element and 48px on coarse pointers; at most two button sizes, at least 6px apart; inputs match the height of their adjacent button (39). Icon-bearing controls take asymmetric padding. Any non-primary form action (change photo, resend code) takes the smaller size and a secondary style. Delete uses a secondary style unless deleting is the page's one action.
- **Links that go nowhere yet.** An action whose URL the brief does not give points at the section of this page that does the most of it (`#lookbook`), and the handoff names the URL it needs; never an invented route.
- **Forms that post nowhere yet.** A form whose endpoint the brief does not give carries an empty `data-endpoint` and never prints a success state: no "Sent", "Reserved" or "You're on the list" for a request that did not happen (70); it says plainly that nothing was sent. The handoff names the endpoint it needs.
- **Inputs.** Constant border width across states; the focus ring is an `outline` with an offset (inset on inputs), never a border change; disabled by three channels together, `opacity: 0.55`, `cursor: not-allowed` and the native `disabled` attribute (39a); reserve `min-height: 1lh` under the field for helper text (F14); 16px on mobile; a `name`, `autocomplete`, the matching `type` and `inputmode` on every field, with a `<label>` or `aria-label`; paste never blocked; submit enabled until the request starts, and a failed submit focuses the first invalid field; an explicit `type` on every `<button>`; a custom chevron on selects; native checkbox, radio and toggle styled by state in CSS, never toggled by script.
- **Type finish.** Hanging punctuation on pull quotes (`::before` with the opening quote translated -100%). Tabular numerals on anything that columns (F6). Balanced heads, pretty body (F3). A testimonial exists only when the user supplied it (46a); its card bottom-aligns the attribution, and the avatar is the real person or nothing.
- **Pricing.** Emphasise a tier with its button and a word, never a different card colour; align buttons across tiers by pushing them to the bottom; a taller featured tier is a grid sibling on explicit rows (`grid-template-rows: <gap> 1fr <gap>`, the featured card spanning all rows), never negative margins.
- **Tables.** Sentence-case heads that never wrap, horizontal rules only, no outer border, no card around a table, full width. On a phone the column that names the row and the one that acts on it stay in view (F25): restack each row as a labelled block, or pin the first column and let the rest scroll. A bare scrolling wrapper hides the amount and the action.
- **Responsive finish.** Body-adjacent UI (meta, nav links, controls, checkboxes) renders larger on mobile and steps down at 60rem; display heads are exempt; body never drops below 16px on a phone. Every desktop sidebar or filter column collapses into a menu or disclosure. Container queries for widgets whose layout depends on their box, placed on the closest wrapper.
- **Browser surfaces** (F16). The scaffold's `base.css` themes the selection, the caret, the scrollbar, the focus ring and the underline; keep them, and set numerals tabular wherever they compare. The parts you did not draw still carry the design.
- **Nav.** One line, 56 to 80px tall; a sticky one taller than the scaffold's `scroll-padding-top` raises it so anchors and focus land clear. Text-only links in the top bar, one weight across default, hover and active (state is a colour or a soft background, never a primary fill); a tab row scrolls sideways rather than wrapping; under 60rem, links that no longer fit go behind a menu, while two or three short ones that fit stay in the bar; the logo is a link home with an `aria-label`.
- **The document.** `lang` on `<html>`, a `<title>` in the page's own words, a one-sentence meta description (F23); heading levels step one at a time and CSS sets their size (F21); every `<img>` carries `width`, `height` and a file that exists (76).
- **Prose pages** (articles, docs): line-height 1.65 to 1.75, one family for the title and its subheads, `text-wrap: pretty` on the title, a measure of 60 to 75ch (25).
- **Stat rows and dashboards.** A stat's title never wraps, no icon sits in a stat, siblings part with a top or side hairline, and a widget's layout answers its container (`@container`), not the viewport.
- **Definition lists.** Features and metadata are a `<dl>`: `<dt>` one weight heavier and higher contrast, `<dd>` regular and quieter, so a link inside the `<dd>` still reads.
- **Compact forms** (sign-in, one field): 20rem wide, never an input and a button sharing one border.
- **Motion finish.** Enters `--ease-out`, exits `--ease-in` (the pair most often reversed); exits at 60 to 75%.
- **Assets.** One icon set; images take an inset outline (`outline: 1px solid <ink at 5 to 10% alpha>; outline-offset: -1px`) instead of a border; `alt=""` when the subject is named beside the image; logos 20 to 28px tall in a footer, footer links at the body weight, footer icons never lighter than `--color-muted`; logo rows split evenly (3 + 3, never 5 + 1) and take the hero's alignment when they sit under it.
